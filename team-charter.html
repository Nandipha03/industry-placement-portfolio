Professional Practice 3

Agile Team Charter

This charter outlines the team's purpose, shared objectives, responsibilities and working agreements for exploring a proposed digital platform to improve ICCV job management at JICS.

Project

ICCV Digital Platform Management

Exploring digital solutions to improve job management, information sharing and operational processes.

Organisation

Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS)

Industry placement and project context.

1. Purpose of the Team

The purpose of the team is to investigate current challenges experienced in ICCV job management and explore how digital solutions could support more organised, accurate and efficient processes.

The team will use the information gathered during the project to understand user needs, identify opportunities for improvement and develop proposed solutions for further consideration.

2. Project Vision

The project vision is to explore a practical digital platform that could help ICCVs manage complaints, capture information, track progress and submit reports more efficiently.

Any proposed solution should consider the needs of its users, the organisation's existing processes and the practical requirements for implementation.

3. Team Objectives

  • Understand the current ICCV job management process.
  • Identify challenges associated with paper-based records and information sharing.
  • Gather and consider feedback from relevant users.
  • Explore possible digital solutions to identified problems.
  • Develop project documentation and proposed requirements.
  • Maintain clear communication and record project progress.

5. Proposed Project Approach

The team will first investigate the current process and gather information about user needs and challenges. The findings will help guide the exploration of possible improvements.

Short-term: Improve Information Access

Explore whether a SharePoint-based approach could make relevant project information easier to access and share. Any approach would need to account for the access and communication challenges experienced by intended users.

Medium-term: Develop a Prototype

Explore a prototype that demonstrates selected functions and allows feedback on usability, navigation and the proposed workflow before further development is considered.

Long-term: Explore an Integrated Platform

Consider the requirements for a more integrated digital platform that could support complaint registration, progress tracking, electronic information capture, reporting and oversight.

These are proposed directions for investigation. They should not be understood as approved, completed or implemented solutions.

6. Communication and Collaboration

The team should communicate clearly, share relevant project information and raise questions or challenges as they arise. Updates and decisions should be recorded where appropriate so that team members can follow the project's progress.

  • Share relevant information with the appropriate team members.
  • Provide progress updates when requested or agreed.
  • Ask for clarification when tasks or requirements are unclear.
  • Record important decisions and agreed next steps.
  • Respect organisational communication and information-handling procedures.

7. Working Agreements

Team members should aim to:

  • Complete agreed tasks within the expected timeframes.
  • Communicate delays or difficulties as early as possible.
  • Respect each other's roles, ideas and contributions.
  • Keep project records organised and accurate.
  • Use feedback to improve project work.
  • Handle organisational information responsibly.

8. User-Centred Approach

The proposed solution should be informed by the experiences and requirements of the people who would use it. The team should consider the existing workflow, access to devices and systems, ease of use, and any practical barriers that may affect adoption.

Feedback should be gathered and reviewed before assumptions are treated as confirmed requirements.

9. Decision-Making

Project decisions should be informed by available evidence, user needs, technical feasibility and organisational requirements. Where a decision requires approval or input from a manager, the relevant matter should be referred to the appropriate person.

Proposed features and changes should be documented so that their purpose and status remain clear.

10. Definition of Done

A project task may be considered complete when:

  • The agreed task or deliverable has been completed.
  • The information is clear, accurate and relevant to the task.
  • Any required review or feedback has been considered.
  • The work has been saved in the appropriate location.
  • The task's progress or outcome has been recorded.

Completion criteria may be refined as the project requirements become clearer.

11. Expected Outcome

The expected outcome is a clearer understanding of current ICCV job management challenges, documented user needs and a set of proposed improvements that can be considered for further development.

The project documentation should provide a basis for discussing potential solutions and their suitability within the JICS environment.

12. Items Requiring Confirmation

As the project develops, the team should confirm the following where applicable:

  • Final agreement on team roles and responsibilities.
  • Communication arrangements and the frequency of updates.
  • Access to relevant users, systems and project information.
  • Approval requirements for proposed solutions.
  • Project scope, priorities and any implementation constraints.

Keeping these items open for confirmation helps ensure that the charter reflects the actual project arrangements rather than unverified assumptions.
