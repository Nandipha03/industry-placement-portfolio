JICS Workplace Project

Agile Team Charter

Establishing a shared direction for improving operational processes through practical, user-centred digital solutions.

PROJECT ICCV Digital Platform Management
ORGANISATION Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS)
DOCUMENT STATUS Working Draft — for review
PORTFOLIO OWNER Nandipha Galada
This charter is a working draft based on the information currently available. Project roles, scope and proposed activities must be confirmed with the relevant JICS project stakeholders before this document is treated as final.

01. Project Purpose

The purpose of the ICCV Digital Platform Management project is to support the digital transformation of operational processes within JICS by reducing unnecessary paperwork and introducing more efficient electronic ways of working.

Some operational activities require forms and documents to be completed on paper before being scanned, stored or shared electronically. This can create additional administrative work and make information more difficult to manage.

The project aims to identify suitable processes for digitisation while retaining physical documentation wherever it remains necessary.

02. Project Vision

The vision is to develop a practical, accessible and user-friendly digital platform that enables ICCVs and relevant JICS personnel to complete suitable operational tasks electronically.

The proposed solution should support electronic information capture, organised record storage and retrieval, document sharing, progress tracking and reduced administrative effort.

The platform should be shaped around users' real working needs, ensuring that the technology is practical, understandable and appropriate for the operational environment.

03. Project Objectives

  • Reduce unnecessary printing, scanning and physical handling of documents.
  • Enable electronic completion, storage and sharing of suitable forms and records.
  • Support electronic signatures where appropriate and authorised.
  • Improve the organisation and retrieval of information through structured digital records and databases.
  • Improve the tracking and management of complaints and inspection-related processes.
  • Explore opportunities to digitise other relevant administrative activities, including procurement-related documentation.
  • Make effective use of available tablets and other suitable technology.
  • Collect user feedback to ensure the proposed solution meets operational needs.

04. Project Team and Stakeholders

Based on the current understanding, the project involves approximately six people: a project representative or lead, two IT personnel and three interns. ICCVs and other relevant operational personnel are important stakeholders because their needs will inform the proposed solution.

Team member or group Expected contribution
Mr Sani Provides project direction and represents the project in broader discussions. His formal title and responsibilities require confirmation.
Two IT personnel Contribute technical knowledge, guidance and input on the feasibility of proposed digital solutions.
Three interns Collaborate to investigate current challenges, analyse requirements, explore possible solutions and contribute ideas from an IT perspective.
ICCVs and relevant operational personnel Provide information about current processes, practical challenges and user requirements, and feedback on the suitability of proposed solutions.

05. Proposed Project Approach

The project is expected to follow a phased approach, allowing the team to explore immediate improvements while working towards a more integrated long-term solution.

Short term — SharePoint solution

Explore a SharePoint-based solution to improve access to and sharing of relevant project information and resources.

Medium term — Prototype development

Develop a prototype to demonstrate the proposed solution, explore user interaction and gather feedback before further development.

Long term — Integrated digital platform

Work towards a more comprehensive platform that integrates relevant operational processes, electronic records, document management and tracking capabilities.

These phases reflect the current proposed direction and may be refined following requirements analysis and feasibility review.

06. Communication and Collaboration

The team communicates through stand-up meetings, face-to-face discussions and email follow-ups. These channels support the sharing of updates, discussion of challenges and coordination of project activities.

Team members are encouraged to contribute ideas, raise concerns and explain their perspectives. Decisions should be informed by constructive discussion and agreement, taking operational needs and technical considerations into account.

Where appropriate, important work should be reviewed together before submission or presentation to identify errors, address concerns and maintain consistency.

07. Agile Working Agreements

The team aims to:

  • Encourage participation and respect each team member's contribution.
  • Discuss challenges openly and consider different perspectives.
  • Base proposed solutions on identified requirements rather than assumptions.
  • Prioritise improvements that address genuine operational problems.
  • Review work before submission and consider relevant feedback.
  • Keep project information organised and communicate important updates.
  • Revisit requirements and proposed solutions when new information becomes available.

08. User-Centred Design and Feedback

The needs of ICCVs and other intended users should guide the project's decisions. The proposed solution must consider their working conditions, access to tablets and ability to use the system comfortably.

Feedback should be gathered and considered during requirements analysis, prototype development and evaluation. The aim is to develop a solution that is not only technically feasible but also practical, accessible and useful in everyday work.

09. Decision-Making and Problem Resolution

Project decisions should consider user requirements, operational priorities, technical feasibility and the intended project objectives.

Team members should have an opportunity to express their opinions and raise concerns. If agreement cannot be reached, the issue should be discussed further and referred to the appropriate project lead or decision-maker when necessary.

Significant changes to the proposed scope or direction should be communicated to relevant team members.

10. Definition of Done

A task may be considered complete when:

  • Its purpose and requirements are understood.
  • The agreed work has been completed and reviewed.
  • Relevant information, documentation or outputs have been recorded and organised.
  • Identified errors and outstanding concerns have been addressed or documented.
  • Relevant feedback has been considered.
  • The appropriate person has reviewed or approved the work where required.

For prototype-related tasks, usability and alignment with the identified requirements should also be considered before the work is accepted.

11. Expected Outcome

The expected outcome is a practical foundation for improving JICS operational processes through digital solutions. By reducing unnecessary paperwork, improving information management and considering user feedback, the project aims to support more efficient administrative work and better access to operational information.

The proposed solution should develop in stages, guided by organisational needs, technical feasibility and feedback from its intended users.
