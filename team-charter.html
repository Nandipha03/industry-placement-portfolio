Agile Team Charter

Establishing a shared understanding of the project purpose, team collaboration, stakeholder involvement and proposed approach to improving workplace processes through digital solutions.

Project ICCV Digital Platform Management
Organisation Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS)

1. Project Purpose

The project aims to investigate how selected operational processes involving ICCVs can be improved through appropriate digital solutions. Some processes currently rely on paper registers, forms and dockets, which may need to be completed, transported, scanned or emailed before information can be accessed by the relevant staff.

The team will explore opportunities to reduce unnecessary paperwork, improve the handling of information and support more efficient document submission, storage and retrieval, while retaining physical documentation wherever it is required.

2. Project Vision

The vision is to work towards a practical and user-friendly digital solution that supports ICCVs and relevant JICS staff in completing work-related processes more efficiently.

The proposed solution should consider the actual working environment, access to technology, ease of use, information security and the needs of the people who will use the system.

3. Project Objectives

The project team aims to:

  • Investigate existing work processes and identify challenges associated with paper-based documentation.
  • Understand how ICCVs complete, submit and access work-related forms and information.
  • Explore opportunities to complete and submit suitable documentation electronically using available technology.
  • Consider appropriate digital document storage and retrieval methods to improve access to information.
  • Explore a prototype that can help assess usability and gather feedback before wider implementation is considered.
  • Consider a longer-term integrated platform, subject to technical feasibility, stakeholder requirements and approval.

Key Stakeholders

ICCVs and relevant operational staff are important stakeholders because they understand the daily processes that the proposed solution is intended to support. Their experiences, requirements and feedback should inform the project's direction.

Other relevant JICS personnel may also be consulted where their input is required to assess operational requirements, technical feasibility, access permissions or information-handling practices.

5. Proposed Project Approach

The project is being considered in three broad phases. These phases represent the current proposed direction and are not confirmation that each solution has been approved or implemented.

Short-Term

Explore a SharePoint-based solution for sharing and accessing relevant project information. Access challenges, including ICCVs' ability to access Outlook or email, must be considered.

Medium-Term

Explore a prototype that allows the team and intended users to assess the proposed workflow, ease of use and suitability before further development is considered.

Long-Term

Investigate the feasibility of an integrated digital platform that could support suitable operational processes and documentation in one coordinated environment.

6. Communication and Collaboration

The team uses a combination of communication methods to discuss project activities, share information and follow up on matters requiring attention.

  • Stand-up meetings: Share updates, discuss progress and identify issues.
  • Face-to-face meetings: Discuss project requirements, ideas and possible solutions.
  • Email follow-ups: Communicate relevant information and follow up on discussions when needed.

Team members should communicate respectfully, listen to different perspectives and share relevant information so that the team can maintain a common understanding of the work.

7. Agile Working Agreements

The team aims to follow these working agreements:

  • Keep discussions focused on the project objectives and the needs of the intended users.
  • Encourage all team members to contribute ideas, ask questions and raise concerns.
  • Work collaboratively and respect the experience and input of other team members.
  • Share progress and communicate challenges as early as possible.
  • Review ideas and proposed solutions before treating them as agreed project decisions.
  • Consider user feedback and use it to guide improvements where appropriate.
  • Handle organisational information responsibly and follow applicable JICS procedures and access requirements.

8. User-Centred Design and Feedback

The proposed digital solution should be informed by the needs and experiences of the people who will use it. A solution may be technically suitable but still be ineffective if it is difficult to access or does not fit existing work processes.

The team should therefore aim to:

  • Understand the current workflow and the challenges users face.
  • Consider whether users have access to the required devices, applications and connectivity.
  • Explore whether forms and documents can be completed and submitted digitally where authorised.
  • Gather feedback on the usability and practicality of any proposed prototype.
  • Consider accessibility, appropriate permissions and the protection of organisational information.

9. Decision-Making and Problem Resolution

The team discusses project matters and considers the views of its members when exploring possible solutions. The aim is to reach a shared understanding and agreement wherever possible.

When a disagreement or challenge arises, the team should clarify the problem, consider relevant evidence and discuss possible options. Issues involving project scope, approvals, technical access or organisational procedures should be referred to the appropriate responsible person.

Proposed changes should be discussed with the relevant project stakeholders before they are treated as confirmed requirements.

10. Definition of Done

A project task or deliverable may be considered complete when the following relevant conditions have been met:

  • The task has been completed according to its agreed purpose and requirements.
  • The outcome has been reviewed by the relevant team members.
  • Any identified issues or limitations have been communicated.
  • Required feedback has been considered where applicable.
  • Relevant documentation or evidence has been updated and stored appropriately.
  • Any necessary review or approval has been obtained before the outcome is presented as final.

11. Expected Outcome

The expected outcome is a clearer understanding of how digital tools could improve selected JICS operational processes. The project should help identify practical opportunities to reduce unnecessary paperwork, improve document handling and support more efficient access to relevant information.

The project findings and any prototype developed should provide a basis for evaluating the proposed approach and determining possible next steps with the relevant stakeholders.

12. Items Requiring Confirmation

The following details should be confirmed as the project develops:

  • The final approved project name, scope and requirements.
  • Specific individual responsibilities beyond each team member's current role.
  • The agreed features and boundaries of each proposed project phase.
  • Technical access, security requirements and any necessary organisational approvals.
  • The method for obtaining and documenting feedback from intended users.
  • The agreed criteria and approval process for accepting completed deliverables.

This charter should be reviewed and updated when the team confirms further project details or agrees to changes in scope.
