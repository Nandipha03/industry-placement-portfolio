User Stories & Tasks

This section presents initial user stories and related tasks for the proposed JICS ICCV digital platform project. The stories explore potential improvements to paperwork, document handling, information submission and tablet usability. They will be refined as the project gathers further information about users' needs and current processes.

Project ICCV Digital Platform Management
Organisation Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS)
Current stage Initial requirements and investigation

1. User Stories

These stories are preliminary and are based on the project's objectives and the topics covered in the planned ICCV survey. They represent needs to investigate, not confirmed findings or completed system features.

US-01 Recording work information

As an ICCV, I want to record complaint and inspection information efficiently, so that unnecessary administrative work can be reduced and records can be organised more effectively.

Related tasks

  • Document the current complaint and inspection recording processes.
  • Investigate where repeated paperwork may occur.
  • Identify information that may be suitable for electronic capture.

Acceptance criteria

Current processes are documented, and potential opportunities for electronic recording are identified for further review.

US-02 Completing documents electronically

As an ICCV, I want to complete suitable work-related documents electronically, so that paper-based steps can be reduced where an approved digital alternative is appropriate.

Related tasks

  • Identify forms currently completed on paper.
  • Investigate tablet applications for editing documents and PDFs.
  • Determine applicable approval, signature and recordkeeping requirements.

Acceptance criteria

Suitable electronic document options and their relevant approval and recordkeeping requirements are documented.

US-03 Including supporting evidence

As an ICCV, I want to associate relevant photographs and supporting documents with work records, so that supporting information can be considered alongside the related record.

Related tasks

  • Investigate the types of supporting evidence used during fieldwork.
  • Review the tablet's ability to capture or attach relevant files.
  • Identify appropriate security and storage requirements.

Acceptance criteria

Evidence-handling needs and security requirements are documented, and a suitable method is proposed for evaluation.

US-04 Submitting work information

As an ICCV, I want an accessible method of submitting work-related information, so that the relevant staff can receive it without unnecessary delays or repeated manual steps.

Related tasks

  • Map the current document submission process.
  • Investigate challenges involving scanning, email and document sharing.
  • Explore submission options and their access requirements.

Acceptance criteria

The existing submission process and its limitations are documented, and possible alternatives are identified for further assessment.

US-05 Retrieving work-related documents

As an authorised JICS staff member, I want to retrieve work-related documents from an organised location, so that I can locate the information needed for my responsibilities more efficiently.

Related tasks

  • Document current methods of storing work-related records.
  • Investigate possible difficulties finding existing documents.
  • Explore suitable storage, retrieval and access arrangements.

Acceptance criteria

Document retrieval needs are recorded, and a proposed approach considers authorised access and recordkeeping requirements.

US-06 Using tablets for work

As an ICCV, I want access to the tablet applications and functions required for my work, so that I can complete suitable tasks with fewer technical difficulties.

Related tasks

  • Investigate access to tablets for work purposes.
  • Identify available applications and functions.
  • Gather information about usability difficulties and missing functions.

Acceptance criteria

Tablet access, available functions and potential usability issues are documented for consideration by the project team.

US-07 Improving current workflows

As an ICCV, I want a digital workflow that addresses difficulties in current work processes, so that I can carry out relevant tasks more effectively.

Related tasks

  • Investigate time-consuming and challenging activities.
  • Identify possible workflow improvements.
  • Compare proposed improvements with collected evidence and project objectives.

Acceptance criteria

Proposed improvements are linked to documented process challenges and reviewed before any implementation decision.

US-08 Understanding user requirements

As a member of the project team, I want to gather and review feedback from ICCVs, so that the proposed solution can be shaped around actual working conditions and user needs.

Related tasks

  • Support the preparation and distribution of the ICCV survey.
  • Address practical access arrangements for intended participants.
  • Review responses and refine the initial user stories when evidence becomes available.

Acceptance criteria

Feedback is collected when distribution becomes possible, findings are documented, and relevant changes to the initial stories are recorded.

2. Project Tasks and Current Progress

The tasks above focus on investigating existing processes, identifying possible improvements and establishing requirements before committing to specific features.

Survey distribution and access arrangements

The ICCV survey has not yet been distributed. The team is working through an access challenge involving organisational email accounts, as email is the intended distribution route. Until suitable arrangements are established and responses are received, the user stories remain preliminary. Survey findings can later be used to validate the proposed needs and refine the related tasks.

3. Reflection

Developing the initial user stories has helped me understand why user requirements should be investigated before a digital solution is designed. Breaking potential needs into smaller tasks provides a clearer way to investigate current processes and consider possible improvements. I have also recognised that gathering requirements depends on being able to reach the intended users. The survey has not yet been distributed because access arrangements for organisational email are still being addressed. At this stage, I need to distinguish between proposed requirements and confirmed user needs. Reviewing the survey responses when they become available will help me refine the stories using evidence rather than assumptions.
