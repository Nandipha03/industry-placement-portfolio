User Stories & Tasks

This section presents initial user stories and related tasks for the proposed JICS ICCV digital platform project. The stories explore possible improvements to document handling, information submission, work processes and tablet usability. They will be refined as further information becomes available.

Project ICCV Digital Platform Management
Organisation Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS)

1. User Stories

Initial user stories and their related tasks

Story ID User Story Related Tasks Acceptance Criteria
◇ US-01 Recording information
As an ICCV, I want to record complaint and inspection information efficiently so that unnecessary administrative work can be reduced.
  • Document current recording processes.
  • Investigate repeated paperwork.
  • Identify opportunities for electronic capture.
 Current processes are documented and potential opportunities for electronic recording are identified for review.
◇ US-02 Electronic documents
As an ICCV, I want to complete suitable documents electronically so that paper-based steps can be reduced where appropriate.
  • Identify forms currently completed on paper.
  • Investigate tablet apps for documents and PDFs.
  • Check approval and recordkeeping requirements.
 Potential electronic document options and relevant approval requirements are documented.
◇ US-03 Supporting evidence
As an ICCV, I want to associate relevant photographs and documents with work records so that supporting information can be reviewed alongside the record.
  • Investigate evidence collected during fieldwork.
  • Review tablet capture and attachment functions.
  • Identify secure handling requirements.
 Evidence-handling needs and security requirements are documented, and a suitable method is proposed for evaluation.
◇ US-04 Submitting information
As an ICCV, I want an accessible way to submit work-related information so that relevant staff can receive it without unnecessary delays.
  • Map the existing submission process.
  • Investigate scanning, email and sharing challenges.
  • Explore submission options and access needs.
 The existing process and its limitations are documented, and possible alternatives are identified for assessment.
◇ US-05 Document retrieval
As an authorised JICS staff member, I want to retrieve work-related documents from an organised location so that I can find information more efficiently.
  • Document current record storage methods.
  • Investigate difficulties finding documents.
  • Explore storage and access arrangements.
 Retrieval needs are documented, with authorised access and recordkeeping requirements considered.
◇ US-06 Tablet usability
As an ICCV, I want access to the tablet applications and functions needed for work so that I can complete suitable tasks with fewer technical difficulties.
  • Investigate access to work tablets.
  • Identify available applications and functions.
  • Gather information about usability difficulties.
 Tablet access, available functions and potential usability issues are documented for the team to consider.
◇ US-07 Workflow improvement
As an ICCV, I want a digital workflow that addresses difficulties in current processes so that I can carry out relevant tasks more effectively.
  • Investigate challenging and time-consuming activities.
  • Identify possible workflow improvements.
  • Compare options with evidence and project objectives.
 Proposed improvements are linked to documented challenges and reviewed before implementation decisions.
◇ US-08 Understanding user needs
As a project team member, I want to gather and review ICCV feedback so that the proposed solution can reflect actual working conditions and user needs.
  • Support preparation and distribution of the survey.
  • Address practical participant access arrangements.
  • Review responses and refine the stories when available.
 Once feedback is received, findings are documented and relevant changes to the initial stories are recorded.

Learning & Development

Reflection

Developing these initial user stories has helped me understand that planning a digital solution involves more than identifying features that could be useful. It requires understanding the work people do, the difficulties they may experience and the requirements that a solution would need to meet. Breaking potential needs into smaller tasks also makes the investigation more structured and manageable.

An important lesson at this stage is that requirements should not be treated as confirmed before sufficient information has been gathered. The survey is intended to help the project team understand the experiences of ICCVs, but its distribution is still being arranged because of organisational email access challenges. This has highlighted the importance of considering communication and access when planning user research. As the project develops, I will be able to review the initial stories against the information collected and reflect on how the findings influence the proposed solution.
