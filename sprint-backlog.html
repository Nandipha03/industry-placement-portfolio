Agile Deliverable 04

Sprint Backlog

This proposed sprint plan outlines the work needed to develop a clearer understanding of ICCVs' operational challenges and use the findings to guide priorities for the proposed digital platform at the Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS).

Project ICCV Digital Platform Management
Organisation Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS)

1. Purpose of the Sprint Backlog

A Sprint Backlog identifies the work selected for a sprint, the tasks needed to achieve its goal and the expected outputs. It helps the team organise its work, monitor progress and identify obstacles that may affect delivery.

For this project, the proposed focus is on understanding the current ICCV workflow and gathering reliable information about the challenges experienced during daily operations. This information will help the team make informed decisions about which problems should receive priority.

Planning note: A formal sprint schedule has not been confirmed. This page therefore presents a proposed sprint plan based on the project's current progress, rather than claiming that a formal sprint has already been completed.

2. Proposed Sprint Goal

Our focus

Establish a clearer, evidence-based understanding of ICCVs' operational challenges so that the team can identify priorities and guide the next stages of the proposed digital platform.

The survey is an important part of this goal because it is intended to collect information directly from ICCVs about their duties, existing processes, administrative challenges and use of digital tools.

4. Dependencies and Possible Obstacles

Some tasks depend on earlier activities being completed. Identifying these dependencies helps the team understand what may delay progress and what needs attention first.

  • Survey access: Organisational email and SIM-card access difficulties may prevent some ICCVs from receiving or completing the survey.
  • Availability of evidence: The team cannot accurately measure how frequently particular problems occur until responses have been collected and analysed.
  • Prioritisation: User stories and backlog priorities may need to change once the team's understanding of the actual pain points improves.
  • Stakeholder input: Proposed solutions will require further discussion and consideration before implementation decisions are made.
Important: No survey findings or response statistics are available yet. The team must use actual responses to support future conclusions rather than treating anticipated problems as confirmed results.

5. How Progress Will Be Evaluated

Progress can be reviewed by checking whether the planned outputs have been produced and whether the team has the information needed to move forward.

  • The survey questions reflect ICCVs' actual responsibilities.
  • A workable method of distributing the survey has been identified and used.
  • Responses have been collected and reviewed collaboratively.
  • Findings are documented clearly and supported by the available evidence.
  • User stories and backlog priorities are reviewed against the identified needs.

These are proposed evaluation points. They will be assessed as the related activities take place.

Learning and Development

Developing this proposed Sprint Backlog helped me understand the importance of breaking a larger project into manageable tasks and identifying the dependencies between them. Preparing the survey is an important step, but the information gathered from ICCVs will be needed before the team can confidently determine the extent of the problems and decide which areas deserve the most attention.

I also learned that documenting progress, obstacles and decisions can help a team remain organised and explain why particular priorities were chosen. Reviewing the evidence together will allow the team to refine its plans instead of relying only on assumptions. This reflects the iterative nature of Agile, where plans can be adjusted as more information becomes available.
