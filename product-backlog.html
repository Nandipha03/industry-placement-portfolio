Product Backlog

The product backlog outlines proposed features and activities for the ICCV digital platform management project at JICS. It provides a structured view of potential improvements and helps the team consider which areas may deliver the greatest value to users.

Project ICCV Digital Platform Management
Organisation Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS)

Understanding the Product Backlog

In Agile, a product backlog is an evolving list of features, improvements and other work that may be needed for a product. Items can be reviewed, prioritised and refined as the team learns more about user needs, technical requirements and project constraints.

For this project, the backlog focuses on exploring how suitable operational processes could move from paper-based handling towards more accessible and organised digital methods, while retaining physical documentation wherever it remains necessary.

Learning & Development

Reflection

Developing this product backlog has helped me understand how Agile planning connects a project's overall vision to smaller pieces of work. Rather than treating every possible feature as equally urgent, the backlog provides a way to organise ideas and consider their potential value to users.

The proposed items still need to be considered against actual user needs and the organisation's requirements. The ICCV survey questions have been reviewed and finalised, but the survey has not yet been distributed. This reminds me that planning must remain flexible until more evidence is available.

My next learning step is to use feedback and information gathered during the project to help refine the backlog. This will support more informed prioritisation and help distinguish between ideas that are useful, technically practical and ready for further consideration.
