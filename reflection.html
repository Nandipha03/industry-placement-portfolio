MY PLACEMENT JOURNEY

Overall Reflection

Looking back on my industry placement at the Judicial Inspectorate for Correctional Services (JICS), I can see how the experience has challenged me to think beyond what I learn in a classroom. It has given me an opportunity to explore how IT knowledge can be applied to real workplace needs, while also learning that professional development takes patience, communication and a willingness to keep learning.

My Experience and Professional Growth

One of the most valuable parts of this placement has been learning to look at a workplace problem from the perspective of the people who experience it. The work surrounding the ICCV job management process introduced me to the importance of understanding existing procedures before suggesting a digital solution.

Exploring how complaints and related information are handled helped me recognise why accurate records, accessible information and clear processes matter. It also encouraged me to think about how technology could support staff without assuming that introducing a digital tool would automatically solve every problem.

Through preparing my portfolio deliverables, I have also gained a better understanding of how a project can be organised into smaller, manageable activities. Instead of looking at a proposed system as one large task, I have been learning to break the work down into requirements, user needs, priorities and planned tasks.

Skills and Knowledge I Have Developed

My learning has not been limited to technical knowledge. I have started to appreciate the planning, reasoning and communication needed to understand a problem and work towards a possible solution.

Requirements Analysis

I have developed a better appreciation for understanding users, their responsibilities and their challenges before deciding what a system should do. This has helped me think more carefully about the problem a proposed solution needs to address.

Agile Planning

Preparing the Agile Team Charter, user stories, product backlog and sprint backlog has helped me understand how project work can be organised, prioritised and broken down into smaller tasks.

Communication and Collaboration

I have learned that project progress depends on clear communication, feedback and coordination with other people. It is important to ask questions, clarify expectations and recognise when information is still needed.

Problem-Solving and Critical Thinking

Considering existing work processes and possible digital improvements has encouraged me to examine problems before jumping to conclusions. I have become more aware of the need to consider practical limitations when proposing solutions.

Challenges and How I Responded

One challenge has been working through the information and organisational arrangements needed to understand the current process fully. Access to communication channels and the availability of the relevant participants can affect how quickly information is gathered. This has reminded me that project work does not always move at the pace we expect.

Preparing the survey questions has also shown me that creating a questionnaire is only one part of gathering requirements. The questions need to be reviewed, the appropriate people need to be reached, and the process needs to be coordinated before responses can be collected. The survey has been prepared, but it has not yet been distributed, so I still need that feedback to strengthen my understanding of the users' needs.

Rather than treating these delays as a reason to stop working, I have continued with the tasks I can progress, including organising the project deliverables and documenting my thinking. This has helped me understand the importance of being adaptable while remaining honest about what has been completed and what is still outstanding.

Evidence of My Development

The deliverables in this portfolio provide evidence of how I have been approaching the project. Each one captures a different part of my learning, from establishing how work should be organised to identifying user needs and reflecting on progress.

01 · Agile Team Charter Demonstrates my understanding of project purpose, team responsibilities, values and working agreements. 02 · User Stories & Tasks Shows how user needs can be expressed as stories with associated tasks and acceptance criteria. 03 · Product Backlog Reflects my learning about organising and prioritising proposed features and project work. 04 · Sprint Backlog Demonstrates how selected work can be grouped around a sprint goal and tracked. 05 · Agile Logbook Records project progress, decisions, challenges and learning throughout the placement.

What I Would Approach Differently Next Time

If I approached a similar project again, I would focus on confirming the project expectations, stakeholders and access arrangements as early as possible. I would also establish a clear plan for gathering requirements, including who needs to be consulted, how feedback will be collected and what needs to happen before the next stage can begin.

I would aim to validate my understanding of the existing process earlier, rather than relying too heavily on initial information. Feedback from the people who perform the work is important because a solution that seems suitable on paper may not fit the realities of their daily responsibilities.

I would also continue documenting decisions and progress as I go. Keeping clear records makes it easier to see what has been completed, identify outstanding work and explain the reasons behind project decisions.

An Experience I Will Carry Forward

This placement has reminded me that learning in a professional environment is not always straightforward. Sometimes progress means completing a deliverable; other times, it means asking questions, waiting for the right information or recognising that there is still more to understand.

I am still developing my confidence and learning how to apply my studies to real workplace situations. I do not expect to know everything already, but I want to keep improving, take feedback seriously and become more thoughtful in the way I approach problems.

Most importantly, this experience has helped me see that working in IT is not only about the technology itself. It is also about understanding people, identifying their needs and considering how technology can make a process more useful and manageable. I will carry that understanding with me as I continue my studies and prepare for my future career.
